Professional summary

I am a solutions architect who enjoys taking a hard problem and designing the answer end to end. Over more than 15 years I have moved from writing the code to deciding what should be built, how the pieces fit together, and how a team delivers it. For the last five-plus years that has been my main work: turning business requirements into scalable, secure, data-driven solutions, and guiding teams to build them.

ProWizard is the clearest example. I have been behind this multi-tenant HCM and staffing platform since 2018 and am its architect today: the tenancy model, the data architecture, the security design, the performance work, and the infrastructure around it. When it needed to onboard customers from legacy systems, I designed the migration pipeline. When a client needed a cloud foundation, I designed their Azure environment.

I work as closely with the business as with the technology: requirements, stakeholders, delivery planning, and governance, through Agile/Scrum, including a team of 12 that I led while working onsite with the client in the United States. I still go deep where it matters, in database design, performance tuning, and code review, and AI has become a growing focus of my work, described below.

Applied AI

AI is changing how software is designed, built, and delivered, and I am giving it growing focus in my work as an architect. I work with leading commercial AI assistants and their APIs, and with AI coding and agent tools, to speed up analysis, design, documentation, and engineering. I also run open models locally with Ollama, including Llama and NVIDIA Nemotron 3.5.

My aim is to bring generative AI into enterprise SaaS products in a way that is practical, secure, and respectful of customer data. That means deciding where a language model genuinely helps, how it connects to existing systems, and where a person still makes the call.

AI-assisted engineering
Using AI assistants and coding agents to move faster on analysis, design, code review, and documentation, while design decisions and accountability stay with me.
Model integration
Connecting .NET applications to language models through HTTP APIs, with conversation memory, and building toward tool calling and retrieval over enterprise data.
Local and private models
Running open models on local hardware with Ollama, which keeps data on the machine and suits privacy-sensitive enterprise work.
Architecture view
Treating AI as part of the solution design: data access, security, cost, and reliability alongside the model itself.
